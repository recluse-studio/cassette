# Minimax residual-access theorem — working proof

Status: proof candidate. Numerical searches test the algebra; they do not establish it.

## Model

Let $R:\mathbb F^q\to\mathbb F^p$ be a residual operator with nonzero columns
$r_1,\ldots,r_q$. Put $G=R^*R$ and $a_j=G_{jj}=\lVert r_j\rVert_2^2$. For a probability
vector $\pi$ with positive entries, draw $J\sim\pi$ and estimate

\[
Rx\quad\text{by}\quad Z_\pi(x)=\frac{r_Jx_J}{\pi_J}.
\]

The estimator is unbiased. Its exact mean-square error is

\[
\mathbb E\lVert Z_\pi(x)-Rx\rVert_2^2
=x^*Q_\pi x,
\qquad
Q_\pi=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{a_j}{\pi_j}\right)-G.
\]

The matrix $Q_\pi$ is positive semidefinite because it is an error covariance form. Define

\[
\nu(R)=\min_{\pi\in\Delta_q^\circ}\lambda_{\max}(Q_\pi).
\]

This minimum is attained in the open simplex. Fix any interior law $\pi^0$. For every retained
column,

\[
\lambda_{\max}(Q_\pi)\ge e_j^*Q_\pi e_j
=a_j(1/\pi_j-1),
\]

so the sublevel set at the value of $\pi^0$ stays a positive distance from every boundary face.
It is therefore contained in a compact truncated simplex, where continuity supplies a minimizer.

Zero columns are deleted before this definition and require neither probability nor access.

## Theorem A — exact minimax law and dual

For the average of $s\ge1$ fresh independent samples,

\[
\min_\pi\sup_{\lVert x\rVert_2=1}
\mathbb E\lVert \overline Z_{\pi,s}(x)-Rx\rVert_2^2
=\frac{\nu(R)}{s}.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\nu(R)
=\max_{\substack{X\succeq0\\\operatorname{tr}X=1}}
\left[
\left(\sum_{j=1}^q\sqrt{a_jX_{jj}}\right)^2
-\operatorname{tr}(XG)
\right].
\tag{1}
\]

Thus, for $\varepsilon>0$ and a nonzero reference $A$, the least integer sample count inside this
estimator class that guarantees

\[
\sup_{\lVert x\rVert_2=1}
\mathbb E\lVert \overline Z_{\pi,s}(x)-Rx\rVert_2^2
\le\varepsilon^2\lVert A\rVert_F^2
\]

is exactly

\[
s_{\min}=\max\left\{1,
\left\lceil\frac{\nu(R)}{\varepsilon^2\lVert A\rVert_F^2}\right\rceil\right\},
\]

when $R\ne0$. If $R=0$, no residual access is required. A nonzero residual with
$\nu(R)=0$ still requires one exact component access; zero variance does not make the residual
resident.

### Proof

The one-sample identity follows by expanding the second moment. Independence and centering divide
the covariance of the sample average by $s$. The supremum of $x^*Q_\pi x$ over the unit sphere
is $\lambda_{\max}(Q_\pi)$, which proves the primal statement and the exact sample count.

For the dual, use

\[
\lambda_{\max}(Q)=
\max_{X\succeq0,\operatorname{tr}X=1}\operatorname{tr}(XQ).
\]

The resulting payoff is convex in $\pi$ and affine in $X$. Restrict first to the compact simplex
$\pi_j\ge\rho>0$ and apply finite-dimensional minimax. For any retained column,

\[
\lambda_{\max}(Q_\pi)\ge e_j^*Q_\pi e_j
=a_j\left(\frac1{\pi_j}-1\right)\longrightarrow\infty
\]

as $\pi_j\downarrow0$, so the primal optimizer remains in the interior. For fixed $X$,
Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\inf_{\pi\in\Delta_q^\circ}
\sum_j\frac{a_jX_{jj}}{\pi_j}
=\left(\sum_j\sqrt{a_jX_{jj}}\right)^2.
\]

Subtraction of $\operatorname{tr}(XG)$ proves (1).

For completeness, the limit in the dual is uniform. Along any sequence $\rho\downarrow0$, the
restricted inner-value functions decrease continuously on the compact density-matrix set to the
continuous displayed dual objective. Dini's theorem permits the limit to pass through the maximum.

### Independently checkable optimality witness

A proposed optimum $(\pi,t)$ can be accompanied by a density matrix $X$ with positive diagonal
entries satisfying

\[
Q_\pi\preceq tI,\qquad
X\succeq0,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}X=1,\qquad
X(tI-Q_\pi)=0,
\]

and

\[
\pi_j=
\frac{\sqrt{a_jX_{jj}}}{\sum_k\sqrt{a_kX_{kk}}}.
\tag{1a}
\]

The first line proves the primal upper bound $\nu(R)\le t$. The dual expression evaluated at
$X$, together with (1a), equals $t$ and proves the reverse bound. The witness therefore proves
global optimality without trusting the optimizer that found it.

Such a witness exists at every optimum. The subdifferential of
$\lambda_{\max}(Q_\pi)$ consists of the coordinate gradients generated by density matrices
supported on the largest-eigenvalue eigenspace. Fermat's rule with the simplex constraint therefore
supplies a density matrix $X$ for which

\[
\frac{a_jX_{jj}}{\pi_j^2}
\]

is constant in $j$. The constant is positive because $\operatorname{tr}X=1$ and every $a_j$ is
positive. Hence every $X_{jj}>0$, normalization gives (1a), and support on the top eigenspace gives
$X(tI-Q_\pi)=0$. This proves necessity as well as sufficiency, including when the top eigenvalue is
multiple.

## Theorem B — two-column closed form

For two nonzero columns, write

\[
a=\lVert r_1\rVert_2^2,\qquad
b=\lVert r_2\rVert_2^2,\qquad
c=\langle r_1,r_2\rangle.
\]

The unique minimax law and its value are

\[
\pi_1^*=\frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt a+\sqrt b},\qquad
\pi_2^*=\frac{\sqrt b}{\sqrt a+\sqrt b},\qquad
\nu(R)=\sqrt{ab}+|c|.
\tag{2}
\]

### Proof

For $t=\pi_1/(1-\pi_1)>0$, the covariance matrix is

\[
Q_\pi=
\begin{pmatrix}
a/t&-c\\
-\overline c&bt
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its determinant $ab-|c|^2$ is independent of $t$. At fixed determinant, its largest eigenvalue is
strictly increasing in its trace $a/t+bt$. The arithmetic--geometric mean inequality minimizes
that trace uniquely at $t=\sqrt{a/b}$. Substitution gives (2).

## Theorem C — orthogonal-column water filling

If the nonzero columns are pairwise orthogonal, $\nu(R)$ is the unique $u\ge0$ satisfying

\[
\sum_{j=1}^q\frac{a_j}{a_j+u}=1,
\tag{3}
\]

and the minimax law is

\[
\pi_j^*=\frac{a_j}{a_j+u}.
\]

For one nonzero column, (3) gives $u=0$.

### Proof

Here $Q_\pi$ is diagonal, with entries $a_j(1/\pi_j-1)$. Requiring every entry to be at most
$u$ is equivalent to $\pi_j\ge a_j/(a_j+u)$. Such a probability vector exists exactly when
the sum of these lower bounds is at most one. The smallest feasible $u$ makes the sum equal one,
which proves the claim.

## Proposition D — the zero boundary and coordinate dependence

In the column model, $\nu(R)=0$ if and only if $R$ has exactly one nonzero column. Indeed, a zero
positive-semidefinite covariance forces $G$ to be diagonal and then forces $\pi_j=1$ for every
nonzero column.

The invariant is homogeneous of degree two and is unchanged by output isometries, column
permutations, and coordinatewise input phases. It is not invariant under general right-unitary
changes. This is necessary: the sampled coordinates are part of the access contract.

## Strict separation from the Frobenius law

The current rule $\pi_j=a_j/\sum_k a_k$ is generally not minimax. For two orthogonal columns with
$a\ge b>0$, its exact worst-query variance is $a$, whereas (2) gives $\sqrt{ab}$. Their ratio
is $\sqrt{a/b}$, which is unbounded even after normalizing $a+b=1$.

This is not a looser constant. It changes which residual description is best. Define the
page-aware minimax distortion

\[
D_A^{\mathrm{mm}}(b_{\mathrm{desc}},b_{\mathrm{meta}})
=\inf_{B\in\mathcal C_{b_{\mathrm{desc}},b_{\mathrm{meta}}}(A)}\nu(A-B).
\]

The Frobenius-optimal description need not minimize this quantity. A Cassette certificate based on
$D_A^{\mathrm{mm}}$ can certify plans that the present Frobenius sufficient bound rejects, while
retaining an exact worst-query mean-square statement inside the declared estimator class.

## Theorem E — exactness criterion for the squared-column-norm law

Let

\[
T=\sum_j a_j=\lVert R\rVert_F^2,
\qquad
\pi_j^{\rm F}=\frac{a_j}{T},
\]

and let $E_{\min}(G)$ be the eigenspace of $G$ belonging to
$\lambda_{\min}(G)$. The squared-column-norm law $\pi^{\rm F}$ is minimax if and only if
there is a density matrix $X$ such that

\[
X\succeq0,qquad \operatorname{tr}X=1,qquad
\operatorname{ran}X\subseteq E_{\min}(G),qquad
X_{jj}=\frac{a_j}{T}\quad\text{for every }j.
\tag{4}
\]

When (4) holds,

\[
\nu(R)=T-\lambda_{\min}(G).
\tag{5}
\]

When (4) fails, the minimax law is strictly better than the squared-column-norm law. Thus (4) is
an exact algebraic test of whether Theorem 5's declared probability law can be improved without
changing its estimator or access model.

### Proof

At $\pi^{\rm F}$,

\[
Q_{\pi^{\rm F}}=TI-G,
\]

so its top eigenspace is $E_{\min}(G)$ and its value is the right-hand side of (5). If $X$
satisfies (4), then

\[
\frac{\sqrt{a_jX_{jj}}}{\sum_k\sqrt{a_kX_{kk}}}
=\frac{a_j/T^{1/2}}{T^{1/2}}
=\frac{a_j}{T}.
\]

The optimality witness (1a) proves that $\pi^{\rm F}$ is minimax.

Conversely, suppose $\pi^{\rm F}$ is minimax. Strong minimax duality supplies a dual optimizer
$X$ forming a saddle point with $\pi^{\rm F}$. Maximization over $X$ forces its range into the top
eigenspace of $Q_{\pi^{\rm F}}$, which is $E_{\min}(G)$. Minimization over $\pi$ forces (1a).
Substituting $\pi_j=a_j/T$ into (1a), then using $\operatorname{tr}X=1$, gives
$X_{jj}=a_j/T$. This proves necessity. Failure of (4) rules out equality, so strict improvement
follows.

## Theorem F — complete rank-one law

Assume $R$ has rank one and at least two nonzero columns. Put

\[
a=\max_j a_j,
\qquad
b=T-a.
\]

If $a\le T/2$, then

\[
\nu(R)=T,
\qquad
\pi_j^*=\frac{a_j}{T}.
\tag{6}
\]

If $a>T/2$, let $h$ be the unique index with $a_h=a$. Then

\[
\nu(R)=2\sqrt{ab},
\tag{7}
\]

and the unique minimax law is

\[
\pi_h^*=\frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt a+\sqrt b},
\qquad
\pi_j^*=\frac{a_j}{\sqrt b(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)}\quad(j\ne h).
\tag{8}
\]

For one nonzero column, $\nu(R)=0$ and its sole probability is one.

### Proof

Write $G=zz^*$ with $|z_j|^2=a_j$. Factor a dual density matrix as $X=V^*V$, and let
$v_j$ be the columns of $V$. After absorbing the phases of $z_j$ into these vectors, put

\[
\ell_j=\sqrt{a_j}\,\lVert v_j\rVert,
\qquad
S=\sum_j\ell_j.
\]

The trace constraint is $\sum_j\ell_j^2/a_j=1$, and the dual objective is

\[
S^2-\left\lVert\sum_j\sqrt{a_j}v_j\right\rVert^2.
\tag{9}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz bounds $S^2\le T$. If $a\le T/2$, choose
$\lVert v_j\rVert^2=a_j/T$. The vectors $\sqrt{a_j}v_j$ then have lengths
$a_j/\sqrt T$. The polygon inequality $\max_j a_j\le T/2$ permits orientations whose sum is
zero. Equation (9) attains $T$, while the squared-column-norm law has covariance $TI-zz^*$ and
top eigenvalue $T$. This proves (6).

Now suppose $a>b$, and write $\ell=\ell_h$ and $L=\sum_{j\ne h}\ell_j$. Cauchy--Schwarz on
the tail gives

\[
\frac{\ell^2}{a}+\frac{L^2}{b}\le1.
\tag{10}
\]

If $\ell\ge L$, the triangle inequality and (10) bound (9) by
$4\ell L\le2\sqrt{ab}$. If $\ell<L$, maximizing $(\ell+L)^2$ under (10) and
$L\ge\ell$ puts the maximum at $L=\ell$; it is $4ab/T\le2\sqrt{ab}$. Thus the dual is at most
the right-hand side of (7).

Set

\[
\lVert v_h\rVert^2=\frac12,
\qquad
\lVert v_j\rVert^2=\frac{a_j}{2b}\quad(j\ne h),
\]

and orient all tail vectors opposite $v_h$. Then (9) equals $2\sqrt{ab}$, proving (7) by the
dual. Formula (1a) gives (8). The constructed witness has positive diagonal, so its reciprocal
inner objective is strictly convex; no second probability law can be optimal.

The same positivity argument proves uniqueness in the balanced case. Theorem E supplies the
equivalent geometry: the squared-column-norm law is optimal exactly when lengths
$a_j/\sqrt T$ close to form a polygon.

## Page-decomposition extension

Let $R=\sum_{g=1}^mR_g$ be any declared decomposition into nonzero, physically fixed,
page-addressable linear components. Sample $g$ with probability $\pi_g$ and return
$R_gx/\pi_g$. Then the same argument gives

\[
Q_\pi=\sum_g\frac{R_g^*R_g}{\pi_g}-R^*R
\]

and

\[
\nu(R_1,\ldots,R_m)
=\max_{\substack{X\succeq0\\\operatorname{tr}X=1}}
\left[
\left(\sum_g\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(XR_g^*R_g)}\right)^2
-\operatorname{tr}(XR^*R)
\right].
\]

This form maps the mathematical sample directly to an aligned page group instead of multiplying a
column count by a scalar-read estimate after the proof.

The page invariant belongs to the declared decomposition, not to $R$ alone. It is zero precisely
when $R_g=\pi_gR$ for every group under some probability vector. The physical page map must
therefore be fixed before optimization; arbitrary algebraic splitting would let a certificate game
the invariant by introducing proportional replicas.

The exact optimality statement concerns fresh independent categorical samples averaged with the
declared Horvitz--Thompson weights. Varying the law between independent samples cannot improve the
worst-query mean-square value, by convexity of $1/\pi_g$. Sampling without replacement can improve
it and requires a separate theorem.
