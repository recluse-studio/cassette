# gaussian_static_oracle_first_chaos_reduction.md — Gaussian first-chaos lower reduction for source-independent static support oracles; depends on ../../MATHS.md and the fixed-linear support notes.

## Statement

Let \(R_0\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\) have

\[
G=R_0^TR_0\succ0,
\]

and let \(U\in\mathbb R^{p\times d}\) have independent
\(N(0,1/p)\) entries. Put \(A=UR_0\). Fix a distribution
\((p_S)\) on supports \(S\subseteq[n]\) with \(|S|\le s\), independent of
both \(A\) and the query \(x\). A static oracle decoder is a Borel map

\[
F_S:\mathbb R^{p\times |S|}\times\mathbb R^n\longrightarrow\mathbb R^p
\]

such that \(F_S(A_{:S},x)\) has finite Gaussian second moment and

\[
\sum_S p_SF_S(A_{:S},x)=Ax
\tag{1}
\]

for every real \(A\) and \(x\). The equality is pointwise exact
unbiasedness for the fixed schedule, rather than an equality only after
averaging over the source.

Write \(E_S:\mathbb R^{|S|}\to\mathbb R^n\) for coordinate inclusion and

\[
M=\sum_Sp_SE_SG_{SS}^{-1}E_S^T.
\tag{2}
\]

Then, for every fixed \(x\),

\[
\mathbb E_{A,S}\|F_S(A_{:S},x)-Ax\|_2^2
\ge x^T(M^\dagger-G)x.
\tag{3}
\]

Under (1) for every \(x\), every coordinate occurs in a positive-probability
support, so \(M\succ0\) and \(M^\dagger=M^{-1}\). The pseudoinverse form
is the correct version when exactness is imposed only for a particular
subspace of queries.

## Proof

Fix \(x\). The finite Gaussian second-moment assumption makes each decoder
an element of the relevant \(L^2\) space.

### Finite signed-permutation symmetrization

Let \(\mathcal H_p\) be the finite group of signed permutation matrices.
Define

\[
\overline F_S(X,x)=\frac1{|\mathcal H_p|}
\sum_{O\in\mathcal H_p}O^TF_S(OX,x).
\tag{4}
\]

This finite average is pointwise defined wherever the original decoder is
defined. It needs no orbit-integrability assertion for exceptional fixed
inputs.
Equation (1) gives, for every \(A\),

\[
\sum_Sp_S\overline F_S(A_{:S},x)
=\frac1{|\mathcal H_p|}\sum_{O\in\mathcal H_p}
 O^T\sum_Sp_SF_S((OA)_{:S},x)
=Ax.
\tag{5}
\]

The averaged decoder is left-equivariant under \(\mathcal H_p\):

\[
\overline F_S(QX,x)=Q\overline F_S(X,x)
\qquad(Q\in\mathcal H_p).
\tag{6}
\]

Convexity of squared norm and the left-orthogonal invariance of the
Gaussian law of \(A\) give

\[
\mathbb E_{A,S}\|\overline F_S(A_{:S},x)-Ax\|_2^2
\le
\mathbb E_{A,S}\|F_S(A_{:S},x)-Ax\|_2^2.
\tag{7}
\]

The schedule is fixed before the source and query, which is exactly what
makes the same weights \(p_S\) available in (4)--(7).

### First chaos

Let \(\Pi_1\) be orthogonal projection onto the first Gaussian Wiener
chaos generated by the entries of \(U\). Gaussian regression shows that
\(\Pi_1\overline F_S(A_{:S},x)\) depends only on the first-chaos span of
\(A_{:S}\): any first-chaos variable orthogonal to that span is orthogonal
to every measurable function of \(A_{:S}\). Hence it is a linear map of
\(A_{:S}\). Projection commutes with the finite group action in (6). Every
linear map \(\mathbb R^{p\times |S|}\to\mathbb R^p\) that is
left-equivariant under \(\mathcal H_p\) has the form

\[
\Pi_1\overline F_S(A_{:S},x)=A_{:S}c_S(x)
=AE_Sc_S(x).
\tag{8}
\]

For completeness, restrict such a map to one input column. It becomes a
linear operator on \(\mathbb R^p\) that commutes with every signed
permutation. Commutation with all coordinate sign flips makes it diagonal;
commutation with all permutations makes its diagonal entries equal.
It is therefore a scalar multiple of the identity. Summing over columns
proves (8); the same conclusion is immediate for \(p=1\).

The target \(Ax\) belongs to the first chaos. Projecting (5) and using
\(G\succ0\) gives

\[
\sum_Sp_SE_Sc_S(x)=x.
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed, the difference has mean-square image norm
\[
\mathbb E\left\|A\left(\sum_Sp_SE_Sc_S(x)-x\right)\right\|_2^2
=\left(\sum_Sp_SE_Sc_S(x)-x\right)^TG
\left(\sum_Sp_SE_Sc_S(x)-x\right),
\]
which vanishes.

Because \(Ax\) is first-chaos, Pythagoras and (8) yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{A,S}\|\overline F_S(A_{:S},x)-Ax\|_2^2
&\ge\sum_Sp_S
 (E_Sc_S(x)-x)^TG(E_Sc_S(x)-x)\\
&=\sum_Sp_Sc_S(x)^TG_{SS}c_S(x)-x^TGx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The final equality uses (9).

### The finite convex program

Minimize the first term in (10) subject to (9). Its Lagrange equations are

\[
G_{SS}c_S=E_S^T\lambda,\qquad
M\lambda=x.
\tag{11}
\]

If \(x\in\operatorname{range}M\), the minimum is
\(x^TM^\dagger x\). If \(x\notin\operatorname{range}M\), (9) is
infeasible. Combining (7), (10), and (11) proves (3).

For pointwise exactness for every \(x\), (9) is feasible for every \(x\).
Equivalently, the positive-probability supports cover every coordinate.
Then

\[
v^TMv=\sum_Sp_S(E_S^Tv)^TG_{SS}^{-1}(E_S^Tv)>0
\]

for every nonzero \(v\), so \(M\) is positive definite.

## Consequence for a static hard-cap schedule

For one fixed schedule,

\[
\sup_{\|x\|_2=1}
\mathbb E_{A,S}\|F_S(A_{:S},x)-Ax\|_2^2
\ge\lambda_{\max}(M^{-1}-G).
\tag{12}
\]

Minimizing the right side over fixed support laws gives the fixed-linear
support value. If it is denoted by \(\nu_s(G)\), then the standard trace
argument gives its water-level lower bound. In fact,
\(\operatorname{tr}(GK_S)=|S|\) for
\(K_S=E_SG_{SS}^{-1}E_S^T\). If
\(M^{-1}-G\preceq vI\), then
\(M\succeq(G+vI)^{-1}\), and hence

\[
s\ge\operatorname{tr}(GM)
\ge\operatorname{tr}\bigl(G(G+vI)^{-1}\bigr).
\tag{13}
\]

Thus \(v\) is at least the water level solving the corresponding trace
equation. For \(|S|\le s\), replace the equality on the left by
\(\operatorname{tr}(GM)=\sum_Sp_S|S|\le s\); the conclusion is unchanged.

Equation (12) is an average-over-Gaussian-sources lower bound. It does not
by itself select a source with a near-isometry property, establish a
finite-word description lower bound, or cover schedules that depend on the
source or query.

## Relation to known methods

The proof is a standard Gaussian average-case information argument:
finite signed-permutation symmetrization removes output-row mixing, and first-chaos
projection removes nonlinear decoder components that cannot improve
mean-square approximation to a linear Gaussian target. The final program is
the familiar principal-inverse design problem. No primary-source check was
performed here, so this note makes no priority or novelty claim and does
not identify a precise prior theorem.
